Before You Begin
You are a client trying to download a 5-slice pizza from a server in Sudan.

The server has 2000ms of latency on every message. You'll go through a Three-Way Handshake, receive five slices (one of which will be lost in transit), and request retransmission of the lost packet. Track your time. The wait is the point.

Goal Experience how the reliability of TCP becomes a hurdle in a high-latency environment. Record your timing data and bring it to the Ethical Brief.

When prompted, you'll need to type the correct TCP response (SYN-ACK, ACK, or a retransmission request). Hints appear above each input. Take it seriously — Omar in Sudan doesn't get to skip the wait.

Reflection — bring this to the Ethical Brief

  1. The Time Cost. How many seconds were "wasted" just waiting for messages to travel back and forth? What part of that wait is latency, and what part is the protocol's design?
  2. TCP vs. UDP. If you were using UDP (no acknowledgments) instead of TCP, how would the Slice #3 situation have been handled differently? What would the trade-off be?
  3. The Divide. Why does a reliable protocol like TCP make the Internet feel broken for a student in Sudan, rather than just "slow"?
