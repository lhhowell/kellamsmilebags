🎯 Essential Question
How do restaurants use data to decide what stays on the menu?

📋 Learning Objectives

Students will:

  • Analyze a real-world restaurant dataset using a spreadsheet.
  • Calculate Profit using the formula Profit = Menu Price − Cost to Make.
  • Apply menu engineering — classify items as Stars, Puzzles, Plowhorses, or Dogs.
  • Justify data-driven decisions about pricing, removal, and new menu items.

💰 The Profit Formula

The core calculation
Profit = Menu Price − Cost to Make
In cell J2: =G2-F2

Walk through it live on the projector. Click cell J1, type Profit. Click J2, type =G2-F2, press Enter. Double-click the small square at the bottom-right of J2 to fill the formula down all 16 rows.

Common Stumble Students often forget the equals sign. If Sheets shows =G2-F2 as text instead of a number, that's the giveaway. Have them delete and retype starting with the = sign.

🎯 Menu Engineering Matrix

Project this 2×2 grid in Module 4. Every item in the dataset belongs in one quadrant based on its profit + popularity.

⭐ STAR
High profit + High popularity
Strategy: Keep & feature.
Examples: Cheeseburger (4.8 / $8.64), Chicken Alfredo (4.7 / $10.39), Chocolate Cake (4.9 / $4.99)
💰 PUZZLE
High profit + Low popularity
Strategy: Rename, reposition, market.
Examples: House Salad (4.2 / $4.39 — solid profit but order count lags)
❤️ PLOWHORSE
Low profit + High popularity
Strategy: Raise price slightly or pair with high-margin sides.
Examples: Soda (4.2 / $2.09 — popular but low absolute profit)
❌ DOG
Low profit + Low popularity
Strategy: Remove from menu.
Examples: Iced Tea (4.0 / $1.94), Veggie Stir Fry (4.1 / lowest entree popularity)

🔹 Module Breakdown

1
Hook · Why do menus change?
5 – 10 minutes

Project: "Why do some menu items stay on a restaurant's menu forever, while others disappear after a few months?" Cold-call discussion. Prompts: Why has the cheeseburger been on the menu since the 1960s? What happens to a salad almost no one orders? If a restaurant LOSES money on every order of a popular item, do they keep it?

Land the Bridge "Restaurants don't just cook — they analyze data. Today you ARE that analyst. You'll look at one restaurant's actual menu and use the data to decide what stays, what goes, what costs more, and what to add."
2
Mini-Lesson · Cost, Price, Profit
10 – 15 minutes

Walk through each column in the Menu Data tab: Cost to Make (what the restaurant spends), Menu Price (what the customer pays), Popularity Rating (avg on a 1–5 scale), Prep Time (minutes per plate). Then model the Profit formula live — =G2-F2 in J2, double-click the fill handle.

3
Guided Practice · Worksheet Parts 1–2
15 – 20 minutes

Students work through Parts 1–2 on their own. Look for: definitions in their own words; a working Profit column (J2:J17) with values between $1.50 and $10.50; identification of most/least profitable items.

4
Application · Menu Engineering · Parts 3–4
20 – 25 minutes

Project the matrix. Students classify each of the 16 items into one of the four quadrants based on profit + popularity. They justify each placement.

5
Real-World Challenge · Fix This Restaurant · Part 5
20 – 25 minutes

Students play consultant. They must: Remove 2 items (justify each with data); Change the price of 2 items (justify with profit + popularity); Add 1 NEW item (defend using the menu engineering matrix).

Strong-Evidence Look-Fors Did they cite specific numbers? ("I removed Iced Tea because profit is only $1.94 and popularity is the lowest at 4.0.") Are price changes proportional ($0.20 on a Plowhorse, not $5)? Does the new item fill a gap? (No vegetarian appetizer? No healthy entree under 500 cal? No premium dessert?)
6
Exit Ticket · Reflection · Part 6
5 minutes

One sentence: a takeaway about data-driven decisions. Strong reflections sound like: "Popular doesn't always mean profitable. Soda is everywhere but barely earns anything." Or: "You can't just remove cheap items — sometimes they're what brings people in."

🟢🔵 Differentiation, Extensions & AI Integration

🟢 Support
  • Pre-fill the Profit formula in cell J2 before sharing.
  • Highlight columns to use (yellow on Cost, Price, Popularity).
  • Sentence frame: "I removed ___ because the profit is ___ and the popularity is ___."
🔵 Extension
  • Calculate profit margin (%) — =J2/G2.
  • Build bar charts of profit by item, then by category.
  • Use =AVERAGEIF() to compare categories.
  • Design "Healthy Menu Section" (under 500 cal) and "High Energy Athlete Meal" (700+ cal).
  • Scatter plot: prep time vs popularity.
🤖 AI Integration · Aligned with VBCPS Rollout
  • Have students use AI to suggest a new menu item that fits a Star profile (high profit + high popularity).
  • Use AI to brainstorm ways to revive a Dog item — a name change, recipe tweak, or better description.
  • Critical rule: Students must verify any AI suggestion against the actual data before accepting it. Use the AI for ideas, the data for evidence.

🍽️ Optional Team Extension

📂 Comparing Restaurants Worksheet · 45 minutes · 3-Student Teams
Three Restaurants, One Dataset
Each student owns one of three restaurants — 🍔 Fast Food, 🍝 Casual Dining, 🌱 Healthy Café — and types their 5-row dataset into a shared Google Sheet. The team picks a comparison (price, calories, or popularity), builds a chart in Sheets, copies it into a 3-slide deck (Title · Chart · Findings), and presents 2–3 data-backed conclusions. A natural follow-up if your students finish the main lesson early or as a separate Day 2.
